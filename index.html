Before you touch a bag

The one rule

No white clothes. Not one.

Clothes get washed about every 4 days on the trip. Anything white in the pile has to go through the machine on its own separate cycle — an extra hour and a half in a laundromat instead of being outside.

Darks and colors only. Navy, black, gray, green, burgundy, brown. Anything marked darks only later is one to check twice.

02

Your cosmetics bag

One bag for everything you put on yourself — your cosmetics bag and your wash bag are the same bag on this trip. One thing to carry in at night instead of two, and one thing to leave behind instead of two.

Anything liquid goes in a sealed zip-top bag inside it. Cabin pressure makes bottles leak, and a leak at the bottom of a suitcase ruins everything packed under it.

It lives in the suitcase and comes into the room every night. It is not the small kit you keep with you on the plane — that one is step 6, and a few things are deliberately duplicated between them.

Two of these still need a list of their own: makeup, and your skin care routine. Send Rich what is actually in each and they will come back here as separate tick boxes, the way everything else on this page works.

03

Count out five days of clothes

Not in the bag yet — on the bed, in piles you can count. Five days covers the four between washes plus one spare for the day something gets soaked at the Fairy Pools.

This is the step that decides whether the suitcase is liveable. Count it now rather than guessing, because you'll only ever open it standing behind a car.

04

The suitcase, in four quarters

Lay the suitcase open and flat with the wheel end nearest you. That end is the bottom; the far end is the top. Keep it that way round for the whole pack, and each thing gets a fixed home you can find without looking.

Roll everything tight rather than folding — rolls take about a third less room, crease less, and will stand on their edge like books so you can see every item from above. Fill in the order of the numbers, bottom half first.

05

Digital packing

Bags done — this is the part that does not go in them. The downloads take hours and they need wifi, so start them running first and work through the rest of this screen while they finish.

Skye and Mull have patchy signal and several of the drives have none at all. Anything that needs to load will simply not load. If it isn't downloaded, it doesn't exist on this trip.

Rich is sending you the itinerary

He'll share the full trip from TripIt — every flight, hotel, ferry and booking, in order, on your phone. Install it and sign in first so the invitation has somewhere to land, then tell him which email address you used.

06

Two bags for the plane

A big one with a little one inside it, so at the gate they count as one. The little one comes out on the plane, lives under the seat, and becomes your day pack for every walk in Scotland.

Some of this duplicates the cosmetics bag on purpose. Those are in the car; this is the kit that gets you through a night flight and a full day without opening the suitcase.

07

Small things, outside pockets

The ones everyone forgets, because they're too small to picture. They go in the outside pockets of the suitcase, so finding a flashlight never means opening the whole thing in a dark parking lot.

08

Wear the bulky things

Boots and a coat eat about a quarter of a suitcase. Wear them instead and the bag shuts easily. You also land in Edinburgh already dressed for the weather, which you'll want at seven in the morning.

09

Door check

Stand by the front door with the suitcase zipped and go through these one at a time. Every one is something that's genuinely hard to fix once you've left.

10

Goal: NOT carrying a huge suitcase upstairs

Nothing left to pack — this last screen is for when you're there. Seven different places to sleep in thirteen nights, and almost none of them have an elevator. The suitcase never leaves the car. Done this way, arriving somewhere takes about four minutes, you each carry one light bag in, and you never unpack the suitcase once.
